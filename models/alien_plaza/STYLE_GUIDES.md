# Style guides, one per chapter

Why this file exists: a level looks good when one cohesive SET of pieces fills every slot on the screen. Weirdport works because its set is complete: a road texture, a sidewalk, a curb, walls with variation, a sky, trim on the edges, accents (AC units, trash, signs), and the living layer (trees, lamps, wires). Glowtide looked wrong because its set was improvised.

So every chapter gets the same checklist, filled with ITS OWN parts. The layout and routes never depend on the set, so a level can be greyboxed, made fun, and dressed last.

## The checklist (every chapter fills every line)

| Slot | What it covers | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Ground A | the main walking surface (road, paving, grass) | the biggest area on screen |
| Ground B | the second surface (sidewalk, sand, dirt) | breaks the first one up |
| Edge | curb, quay lip, ledge, roof rim | where two surfaces meet; the eye follows edges |
| Wall | 2 to 4 wall materials in ONE palette, plus one base course | the second biggest area on screen |
| Trim | window frames, cornices, door frames, posts | separates wall from roof and ground; reads as "built" |
| Roof | the roof surface and its rim | seen from above on every rooftop route |
| Openings | windows, doors, shutters, awnings | the rhythm of a facade |
| Accents | 4 to 6 small props that mean this place | the things the eye lands on at human height |
| Living | trees, bushes, grass, weeds, lamps, wires, banners | makes it feel inhabited |
| Sky + light | the sky dome and the sun colour | the mood; also the only thing behind everything |
| Signature effect | ONE effect the level is known for | water, smoke, blowing grass, dust, fog |

Rule: a new piece is rendered next to a reference shot before it ships. If it does not hold up beside the reference, it does not go in.

## How a chapter's set gets made

1. Greybox the level and prove its routes (the checker).
2. Pick the reference shots (three or four images that are the mood).
3. Fill the checklist above for the chapter.
4. Make the ground, edge, wall and sky first; render against the reference; fix until it holds up.
5. Then trim, roof and openings. Then accents and living. Then the signature effect.
6. Only then dress the level.

Glowtide is at step 1 with a borrowed set (the city's) to test the layout. Its own set starts at step 2.
